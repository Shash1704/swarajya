# Swarajya

A real-time grand-strategy game set in India in **1670**, the year Shivaji began retaking the forts he had surrendered to the Mughals at Purandar. You lead the Marathas from Raigad against the Mughal Empire, the Deccan sultanates and the coastal powers. As in Mount & Blade: Bannerlord, the campaign map runs continuously (pause any time): every realm marches, fights, besieges and plunders at once. Battles and sieges are fought in real time with pause.

## Look

Styled after painted history-map videos (such as History Marche): realm colours laid over shaded relief, pale seas with glowing coasts, realm names in spaced Roman capitals (Cinzel), and towns as ink dots. Every army marches under a named commander's standard (Shivaji Bhonsle, Diler Khan, Ali Adil Shah…). On the battlefield, regiments are square two-tone counters carrying their military symbol, the commander's standard flies over his regiment, and the place name is written across the field.

## Run it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open the URL Vite prints. Add `?seed=123` to fix the random seed (terrain detail and battle layouts), or `?debug` to expose the game as `window.banners` in the console.

## The campaign

- **Time:** the world clock runs from 1 January 1670. **Space** pauses or resumes, and **1/2/3** set the speed (1 day per second at 1×). Income and recruitment are monthly. The monsoon (June to September) slows every army, stalls siege works, wears down armies camped in hostile land, and spoils bows and matchlocks.
- **Map:** southern Asia from Persia to the Malay world, with 199 historical provinces: Safavid Persia (Isfahan, Tabriz, Shiraz, Hormuz, Khorasan, Herat and Kandahar), Afghanistan (Mughal Kabul, Ghazni and Jalalabad; Balkh, Badakhshan and the Hazarajat), the Indian subcontinent, Toungoo Burma and Arakan, Ayutthaya's Siam and Burmese-held Lan Na, Lan Xang, Cambodia, Dai Viet under the Trinh and the Nguyen lands to the south, the Malay sultanates and Dutch Malacca, and Aceh in Sumatra; Arabia, Ottoman Iraq, Bukhara and Qing China lie beyond the frontiers. New realms: the Persians, the Burmese, the Siamese, Dai Viet, Aceh and the Dutch. Rivers, the Ghats, the Himalaya, the Zagros, the Alborz, the Hindu Kush and the Annamite chain, in **3D**: real relief from the map's elevation (the Himalaya, the Iranian plateau, the Ghats, the Deccan plateau), the painted map with realm colours, borders and rivers draped over it, a rippling sea, and little 3D forts, towns and ports at every settlement, with a company of soldiers in its colours marching under each army's banner. Drag to pan, scroll to zoom, **Z/C** or the ⟲ ⟳ buttons to turn the view, **Shift+drag** to turn and tilt it. The **3D/2D** button in the top bar switches to the flat painted map; the choice is shared with battles and remembered per browser.
- **The living map (3D):** as on Bannerlord's world map, the land is shown in its own colours, with the realms as a light wash and a band of their colour along their frontiers. Tens of thousands of single trees make the forests (jungle and sal in the wet country, deodar and pine in the hills, palms on the coasts, thorn scrub in the dry country); snow lies on the Himalaya. Towns stand behind walls and towers with packed houses, flat roofs and tiled ones, a domed mosque or palace and minarets; ports have quays and boats; hill forts have bastions and a keep; villages are thatched huts and haystacks among their fields; dirt roads join neighbouring towns. A flag in the owner's colours flies over every town and fort, and every army marches under its standard. The light turns from day to dusk to night while the world runs, with lamps lit in the towns after dark, and clouds drift over the land when you look from high up. You can zoom much closer than before.
- **Orders:** select an army and right-click a town to march there, right-click anywhere else to march straight across country to that spot, right-click an enemy army to hunt it down, or right-click one of your own armies to join it. Armies move freely over land: they go round the sea, prefer passes and valleys to high ridges, and are slowed by hill country. All-horse armies march fastest; elephants and guns slow everyone down. The date bar also counts the days since the campaign began.
- **Abandoned forts:** an army beaten in battle near one of its own forts takes the garrison with it as it flees. The fort stands empty for about three weeks, with no new garrison, and the victors can walk in. The AI is quick to do so.
- **Encounters:** when a hunter catches its prey, or an AI army likes its odds, battle is joined. Every friendly or allied army within reach marches to join, and an army caught at its own town fights beside the garrison, so battles can grow to dozens of regiments a side. Sieges work the same way: nearby allies join the assault, and armies inside a town help defend it. If you are involved the game pauses and you choose: command the battle, auto-resolve it, or escape (with a rearguard lost if you are slower). AI-versus-AI battles play out on the map under a crossed-swords marker.
- **Sieges:** arriving before an enemy town opens a choice: besiege it, storm it if it has no walls, or raid its villages. A siege runs day by day: the garrison eats its food, and your camp builds ladders (4 days), then a ram (12 days). Assault whenever you like (every 2 days), or wait for a starving garrison to surrender. Mavalas bring ladders from day one.
- **Taking towns:** a captured town can be **occupied** (steady income) or **sacked** (a fortune now, devastated for half a year).
- **Bajirao's war: ride light, harass, encircle.** An all-horse army can **ride light** (🐎 on your army card): it leaves its baggage behind, marches a third faster and lives off the land, but carries only 12 days' food and cannot lay a siege. Open an enemy army's card and press **🐎 Harass** to shadow it just out of its reach: your horse take its convoys and burn its forage. Cut off in the field, it eats through its stores, then starves and its men desert, and after a few days of starving it asks for terms: accept, and its army lays down its arms and its commander is your prisoner, without a battle. An army that reaches one of its own towns is fed and safe, so catch it far from home; anything slower than you can't bring your harassing horse to battle. When the fight comes, an army cut off or starving comes to it hungry and low in heart, and if your armies (or the Raje's commanders) close on it from different sides, the battle is fought with it surrounded in the middle of the field, each of your armies on the side it came from. The Raje's commanders harass stronger Mughal armies the same way.
- **Raids (chauth):** plunder an enemy province's villages for 2 days for gold. It then pays its owner nothing for two months.
- **Villages:** every province has two or three villages (huts on the map, names when zoomed in, listed in the province tooltip). Right-click one to ride there, then press **🔥 Sack** on your army card: a day's plunder brings gold and food, but the village burns for five months, the province grows restless, and word spreads. The owner's armies and every Mughal army within reach ride out to hunt the raiders. AI armies sack villages too.
- **The empire's many fronts:** each Mughal army keeps to one front (the Deccan, Gondwana, Rajputana, Gujarat, Awadh, Bengal and Assam, the north-west), so the empire is busy all over the subcontinent. No realm sends more against the Marathas than about half as many armies as the Marathas field, or much more than their strength, so a young Swarajya faces fights it can win; the full weight comes as it grows. The Raje's commanders fight the Maratha way, raiding more than they besiege. Great hill and sea forts never give up just because a battle was lost nearby.
- **Realms in 1670:** Marathas (you), Mughals, Bijapur, Golconda, the Siddis of Janjira, the Portuguese, the English, the Rajput states, Mysore, the Nayaks of Madurai, the Ahoms, and independent chiefs. You start at war with the Mughals and Siddis; the AI declares wars and makes peace on its own.
- **Regions, building and diplomacy:** hold a whole region for +25% revenue there. Build walls or markets (one project a month per town). Declare war or send envoys for peace.
- **Manpower:** every regiment is drawn from a realm's pool of fighting men, which regrows each month from provinces that are not devastated or besieged. Recruiting, refilling ranks and rebuilding garrisons all use it up; regiments are raised under-strength when men are short. A realm that bleeds its manpower dry sues for peace, accepts it readily, and its garrisons start opening their gates.
- **Supply and attrition:** armies carry a month of food (more under a quartermaster). In friendly country they refill; in enemy country they forage, which rich untouched land allows and ravaged land doesn't. Out of food, they starve. Raiding feeds an army.
- **Hill forts:** your forts feed and refill armies nearby, and ranks fill twice as fast there. Enemy forts' guns harass and slow armies on the passes. Each region has a key fort; its holder gets +15% revenue and +25% recruits across the region, and unrest where it's held by an enemy.
- **Chauth and sardeshmukhi:** at an enemy town (or during a siege), demand chauth. If they're afraid enough, the province pays a quarter of its revenue to you for a year, plus a lump sum now. If you already hold land in the region, sardeshmukhi adds another tenth. Plundering a tributary ends the deal. AI raiders may demand chauth from your towns.
- **Unrest and faith:** every province has unrest, driven by foreign rule, rulers of another faith, taxes, plunder, famine and recent conquest. Garrisons, temples and a tolerant policy calm it. Over 50 the province pays half, over 70 it sends no recruits, and at 80+ it may revolt: the deshmukhs go independent or declare for the realm whose people they are. The Desh and Konkan are Maratha country, so they resent Mughal and Bijapuri rule. Set your taxes (light, normal, heavy) and faith policy (tolerant, zealous) in the realm panel.
- **Commanders and court:** commanders have a trait (bold, cautious, horseman, siege master, raider, quartermaster) and gain rank from battles; both shape battles, sieges, marches and plunder. One whose regiment breaks may be killed or captured. Ransom your own back, or sell enemy captives back to their realm. Keep them loyal: an empty treasury or a refused request for land (a jagir) sours them, and a commander below 15 loyalty may defect with his whole army.
- **Diplomacy:** relations between courts; alliances (allies join your wars), marriage alliances, hostage exchanges (two years of sworn peace; breaking it is a disgrace), gifts, paying one realm to attack another, and peace on your terms: white peace, tribute, or a ceded province. Beaten AI realms offer land as well as gold.
- **Navy:** squadrons at the ports (Siddis, Portuguese, English, Mughals, and a young Maratha fleet at Sindhudurg). Raid enemy coasts for plunder, blockade a port (its trade dies and a besieged sea fort can't be fed by boat), or patrol your coast (+15% trade, intercepts raids). Squadrons fight whoever they meet. Build shipyards and ships in coastal provinces.
- **Buildings:** walls, markets, temples (calm unrest), stables (cheaper, seasoned cavalry), granaries (bigger stores, full wagons, no famine) and shipyards.
- **Spies:** scout an enemy army (otherwise you only know its rough size until you're close), sabotage siege works, bribe a besieged garrison to open its gates, or incite unrest in an enemy province. Enemy agents burn your siege works too. Besieged garrisons make night sorties to burn the besiegers' ladders and rams; you can order one from your own besieged towns.
- **Events:** the coronation at Raigad, a summons to the Mughal court, famines, sickness in camp, European traders, the blessing of Samarth Ramdas, defecting mansabdars and buried treasure, each with a choice.
- **You:** a nobody, as in Bannerlord. At the start you name your character and choose where you come from (Mavala hillman, deshmukh's younger son, silahdar horseman, fort carpenter's son), which sets how you fight. You begin as a captain with a handful of men in the service of Shivaji, who stays the Raje at Raigad. You lead one army, your own; every other army, the Raje's commanders' included, moves on its own: each campaigns independently, conquering, raiding, sacking villages and riding circuits of its front instead of sitting idle. Win battles to rise in rank and renown; your honour (keeping your word) counts with everyone. If your army is destroyed, you escape and gather a few men again.
- **Characters:** every commander and sovereign is a character with a temperament (the famous ones as history remembers them: Shivaji, Aurangzeb, Diler Khan, Siddi Qasim, Gerald Aungier, Tanaji…), an opinion of you, and a memory of what passed between you.
  - **Go before the Raje** (Court): report your deeds for a reward, ask for men (two regiments of Mavalas when you've shown your worth), or hear his counsel.
  - **The Raje's commanders:** talk with them anywhere: share your spoils, ask their counsel, or ask one to join forces with you (his army marches to yours and merges, if he respects you and your name stands as high as his).
  - **Parley** with any army near one of yours, even when battle is about to be joined: demand they withdraw, demand surrender (the commander becomes your prisoner), agree to pass in peace, offer a gift, or invite the commander into your service with his army.
  - **Audiences** with rulers (Diplomacy): talk, then propose peace (white, for tribute, or for a province), alliance, marriage or hostages.
  - **Depth without an AI:** characters understand what you say (forgiving misspellings): intentions, and the places, armies, people and realms you name. They answer from the real state of the world: ask about "the Mughal army near Ahmednagar" and they size it up (who leads it, how many would really fight with the garrison and armies close by, how that compares with your forces) and give their view as their temper dictates. Each has his own aim (Tanaji has sworn to take Kondhana) that he raises, asking whether you'll ride with him, and he understands your yes or no. They have opinions of the people you mention (rivals as history remembers them).
  - **One banner:** ask one of the Raje's commanders to join forces, to attack an enemy army with you, or to march on a town, and his army falls in under your banner, as in Bannerlord. The host marches as one at the pace of its slowest army, shows a single banner with the combined numbers ("with Tanaji"), and fights together (you command both in battle). He goes back to his own war after a couple of months, or when you release him from your army panel.
  - **Natural talk:** characters greet you according to whether you have met, what they have heard of your deeds and their mood. Ask them who they are, how the war goes, what they think of their master (a resentful commander may let slip that he would serve a better one), what news there is, what men say of you, or about your last meeting. Insults, threats, flattery and talk of gold each get the answer that suits the man's temper. Say an offer in plain words ("withdraw or die", "join me") and it's put on the table to make formal. Enemy commanders call out to you before battle.
  - Type anything you like. Words shift the character's opinion a little (charm works only so far, and once a month); the formal proposals are decided by the game's rules, with that opinion counting, and the character then answers in his own words.
  - **Turn on AI, in one click:** every conversation without an AI offers **🗣 Turn on AI**, which finds what your computer has: Chrome's built-in AI (Gemini Nano; nothing to install, though Chrome downloads its model once, about 2 GB, after asking), a model server already running (Ollama or LM Studio, picking a model it has), or else a small model run inside the browser (about 1 GB, after asking). Where Chrome's AI is ready, characters use it from the start.
  - **Local AI (🗣 AI button):** characters can be voiced by a language model on your own computer: a local server with an OpenAI-style API (Ollama: `OLLAMA_ORIGINS="*" ollama serve`, then `http://localhost:11434/v1`; or LM Studio), or WebLLM running in the browser on the GPU (WebGPU; the model downloads once, about 1–2 GB). Nothing is sent anywhere else. With no AI set up, characters answer with written lines and everything still works.
- **Historical battles:** from the start menu, fight Gaugamela (331 BC: Alexander against Darius III), Uhud (625: the Muslims of Medina against the Quraysh), Yarmouk (636: Khalid ibn al-Walid against the Byzantines under Vahan), Palkhed (1728), Delhi (1737) and Bhopal (1737: Bajirao rings the Nizam's guns by the Upper Lake), on either side: pick a side and the field turns so your army starts at the bottom. After each you learn how it really went, and whether you won as history did or changed it. Palkhed and Delhi are fought as Peshwa Bajirao I or his enemies. Each is fought on a map of the real ground (at Palkhed, after the period battle map: the ridge to the north, the Shiv nadi to the east, the Godavari with Puntamba ford where the Nizam's great guns were left behind, and the four Maratha divisions of Pilaji Jadhav, Bajirao, Davalji Somvanshi and Holkar closing round him; the Delhi Ridge, the Talkatora tank, Rikabganj and the road to Shahjahanabad), with its historical armies scaled to the game's regiments: an all-cavalry Maratha army, with horse archers whose bows outrange the matchlock to harass the enemy ranks, against the Nizam-ul-Mulk's hollow square of matchlocks, light guns, elephants and Mughal sowars (hungry, parched and already low in heart after weeks of Bajirao cutting his convoys), or against Mir Hasan Khan Koka's sally from Delhi with the Emperor's household cavalry, with the Vizier Qamar-ud-din Khan's army coming up from the east 90 seconds into the fight: break Koka before then, or get away as Bajirao did. Palkhed is won as it was, by the ring rather than a charge: the Nizam's camp starves minute by minute until his army breaks and he asks for terms, while a frontal assault on his far larger body of foot is costly. He wins if his own regiment breaks out to the edge of the field, and his great guns march up from Puntamba to relieve him if they are not cut off. In the dry season the rivers are low and fordable anywhere, though men in the water are easy prey for a charge. Every regiment starts where it stood, named for its commander (Holkar's, Shinde's, the Pawars'). You may re-form your regiments before you begin. Afterwards, the game tells you what really happened.
- **The Chronicle (📜):** the record of your life, kept like a Maratha bakhar: every battle you fight (where, against whom, the numbers on each side and the losses), sieges, towns taken and sacked, raids and chauth, enemy commanders captured or slain, bargains struck at parleys and audiences, wars declared and peace made, your promotions, your escapes, and the great events of the realm, with a tally of your deeds. Characters have heard of what's written there.
- **Victory:** hold 35 provinces or take Delhi. The triumph is celebrated, and then the campaign goes on: keep conquering, or hold what you have won against the realms that will try to take it back. Only the fall of Swarajya ends the game.

## Battles

Battles are real-time with pause on a large field, drawn in **3D** (Babylon.js): the painted battle map is draped over real hills, with woods of trees, village houses, towered siege walls that crumble as they're breached, and every regiment shown as a block of little soldiers (foot, horsemen, elephants with howdahs, guns with crews) in its colours, with its counter floating above. Sun, shadows, haze, morning mist and dusk light the field. Ground, walls and houses are textured (grass grain, dressed stone, lime plaster), with the textures generated in the browser rather than downloaded. **Z/C** (or the ⟲ ⟳ buttons) turn the camera and **Shift+middle-drag** turns and tilts it. The **3D/2D** button switches to the flat map view (remembered per browser; browsers without WebGL use 2D). Scroll (or pinch) to zoom; use WASD, the arrow keys or middle-drag to pan. First there is a deployment phase in which armies form up in ranks; press **Space** to begin, and Space again to pause at any time. Use **1/2/3** for speed. An army holds up to 20 regiments.

- **Horse on the unready:** cavalry falling on a regiment that is caught out (marching or falling back instead of standing to receive them, already locked in a fight with another enemy, or softened: shaken, spent or cut down to half) is not stopped by its spears: the charge goes home and the horsemen cut it down far faster, the more of those things at once, the worse. A formed, ready line still breaks a head-on charge. The enemy's horsemen look for such prey.
- **High ground and standing still:** attacks from well above the target (arrows, shot and blades alike) hit up to twice as hard. A regiment standing still takes more harm from arrows and shot than one on the move.
- **Water:** a regiment wading a river or tank, or crossing at a ford, has no footing and no ranks. Every blow against it lands harder, horse count it as caught, and a charge into it hits almost twice as hard, more for horse, and shakes the men badly. Spearmen charged by heavy horse in a river lose about four times as many men as on dry ground.
- **The battle meter:** a bar across the top of the field shows whose favour the battle is going in (the strength still on the field: men, quality, nerve and freshness). The side that is winning fights better and holds its nerve; the losing side wavers. When the enemy's share falls below a fifth (the dashed mark), his whole army breaks and his commander asks for terms. An army also breaks early if your regiments surround it (no gap of more than a third of the circle) while the battle goes your way. You then choose: accept the surrender (his men lay down their arms and go home, most of them lost to him, and he is your prisoner); destroy the army (surrounded, it is cut down to the last man and its commander most likely dies with it; in the open most fall and a few escape); or ride them down yourself. Commanders fight with their regiments: an enemy general in the thick of the fighting may be slain outright, and his army's nerve goes with him.
- **Cheat:** in any battle, press **⌘ Command + Shift** together (Ctrl + Shift on Windows and Linux) to strike down the whole enemy force and win at once.
- **Downhill charges:** a charge down a slope gathers speed and carries further, hits up to 2.2 times as hard for horse (1.6 for foot), shakes the men it strikes, and breaks into a formed line with fewer losses to the riders. Cavalry coming down a hill can ride over spearmen it could not touch on the flat.
- **Terrain decides battles:** hills give the high ground (stronger blows, longer range, downhill charges; uphill marches are slow); rivers can be crossed safely only at the sandy fords; marshes bog down horse and elephants; villages and woods give cover. **Right-drag** (or long-press and drag) to draw a curved path for the selected regiments; they wheel through gentle turns, and a sharp change of direction (more than about 70°) is an instant about-face, so a regiment ordered back turns on the spot and marches straight off.
- **Battle lines at any angle:** **Shift+right-drag** (or press **Q** for line mode, also a button in the Orders panel) draws a straight battle line. The selected regiments spread along it, in ranks if there are more than fit (foot in front, then shooters, horse and guns), and wheel to face square to it: drag left to right to face up the field, right to left to face down. During deployment, right-drag always draws a line. Regiments keep the facing they were given until an enemy comes close.
- **Disengaging:** order a regiment that's locked in melee somewhere else and it breaks off with "Falling back!". For a few seconds it doesn't fight back and the enemy strikes its rear, and it loses a little morale. Horse gets away from foot; slow troops chased by fast ones may be caught again. The AI pulls out cavalry that's stuck against spears, and regiments about to break.
- **Field battles:** terrain comes from the province (forest, hills, desert, plains, rivers, marsh). Flank and rear attacks deal extra damage, charges hit hard, and spears brace against horse and elephants. Morale breaks, and routs spread to nearby units.
- **Flanks, charges and commanders:** a regiment fighting to its front holds its facing and wheels only slowly, so pinning it and striking its flank or rear pays: infantry deal far more damage on a flank, and cavalry charges only work from the side or behind. Head-on into formed infantry the charge breaks and the riders take losses, spears worst of all. Archers are cavalry's natural prey. Each side's commander rides with its strongest regiment; if that regiment routs or dies, every regiment on that side takes a heavy blow to morale.
- **Fog of war:** you see only what your regiments can see. Light horse scout furthest and high ground helps; ridges block sight; woods and villages hide anyone who keeps still in them. Enemies out of sight leave a fading "?" where they were last seen. The AI plays by the same rules.
- **Ganimi kava:** a regiment that lies unseen for a few seconds and then strikes springs an **ambush**: a heavy shock to the target's morale and harder blows for a few seconds (Mavalas most of all). Cavalry can **feign flight** (F): they gallop off as if beaten, enemies who give chase lose their order and are easier to cut up, and after a few seconds the riders wheel about. The AI hides hill troops and light horse in woods when it defends.
- **Formations:** open order, tight block (holds charges and steadies morale, but slow, clumsy in woods, and shot to pieces by guns), loose order (arrows do far less harm and woods are easy going, but a charge rides straight through), spear hedge (cavalry and elephants break on it, but it can't move), and cavalry wedge (a much heavier charge, thin flanks). Keys N, T, L, B, G or the Orders panel.
- **Fatigue:** marching, running, climbing and fighting tire regiments; tired men move slower, hit softer and break sooner. Resting recovers them.
- **Ammunition:** archers and gunners carry limited arrows and powder and must go back to the baggage at their own edge of the field to refill. Garrisons on their walls draw on the fort's magazine.
- **Elephants panic:** rockets, guns and spear points frighten them; past a point they run amok through whoever is near, their own side included, until they calm or the mahout kills the beast.
- **Rally and veterans:** routed regiments near the commander's standard rally sooner, and twice. **R** sends the commander along the line: nearby regiments steady and routed ones turn back (every 45 seconds). Regiments earn experience (Seasoned, Veteran, Elite chevrons) that makes them steadier and deadlier; raw recruits dilute it.
- **Weather and time of day:** rain brings mud and wet powder; morning mist cuts sight until it lifts; a battle joined late in the day ends at nightfall in a draw if neither side has broken.
- **Commanders:** each side's commander shapes the battle through his rank and trait (bold charges, cautious defence, horseman's cavalry, and so on).
- **Sieges:** walls, a gate and towers that shoot attackers. Guns and elephants batter walls; infantry hack at the gate (much faster with a ram); ladders let infantry climb. The attacker wins by breaking the garrison, or by holding the town square.

Every unit is marked with a NATO-style map symbol: a cross for infantry, a single diagonal for cavalry, a dot for gunpowder, and an oval for armour (elephants, armoured horse). Modifiers mark the type: a mountain for hill troops, a spearhead for pikes and lances, a bow arc for archers. A double frame marks guard troops. The **Troops** button on the campaign map opens the full tier list.

| `src/campaign/chronicle.ts` | The chronicle of the player's deeds |
| `src/campaign/dialogue.ts` | Written dialogue: reading what was said, aims, assessments and replies built from the world |
| `src/campaign/talk.ts` | The conversation window and the AI settings |
| `src/llm.ts` | Local language models: an OpenAI-style server (Ollama, LM Studio) or WebLLM in the browser, with streaming |
| `src/pref3d.ts` | The 3D/2D preference and WebGL check (no Babylon, so it stays out of the main bundle) |

It's TypeScript built with Vite. The campaign map and the battlefield are 3D with Babylon.js, loaded after the page appears so the game starts at once; names, banners, orders and effects are drawn on a 2D canvas laid over the 3D view, which also takes all input. Everything still works in 2D.
